Applied AI Practice

Use the tool to solve a production problem—not to create a new one.

My hands-on work focuses on the gap between an impressive demo and something a creative team can actually use, review and repeat.

Visual Development

Generate and refine references, look exploration, environments, shot concepts and previsualization while protecting the creative brief.

Reference Control

Use character, environment, style and shot references to reduce drift and keep output aligned across a sequence rather than treating every generation as an isolated image.

Prompt & Model Evaluation

Compare outputs, isolate failure modes, revise prompts and choose tools based on the production need rather than loyalty to a single model.

Quality Control

Evaluate composition, continuity, realism, brand or story alignment, cultural accuracy and usability—not simply whether an output looks impressive at first glance.

Human Review

Keep clear review gates for creative judgment, factual verification, rights, participant dignity and final approval.

Workflow Design

Turn successful experiments into named steps, reusable references, documented criteria and handoffs that a broader team can repeat.

A Practical Workflow

From creative intent to repeatable output.

Turn the proven pattern into a reusable workflow, handoff or team practice.

Workflow Example

Narrative previsualization and shot development.

An independent narrative-production workflow became a practical test bed for the same issues enterprise teams face when they try to move generative AI from isolated experiments into consistent creative output.

The production challenge

Creating cinematic stills and motion concepts across a sequence requires more than a strong one-off image. Characters, environments, lens behavior, composition, cultural details and narrative continuity need to remain coherent from shot to shot.

The working approach

  • Define character and environment reference assets before generating a batch.
  • Translate shot intent into camera, lens, composition and action requirements.
  • Use image generation for controlled keyframes and previsualization.
  • Compare motion tools according to the needs of a specific shot.
  • QC outputs in batches for continuity, realism, composition and reference adherence.
  • Regenerate selectively rather than accepting drift or scaling a broken workflow.

What the failures taught me

AI output can degrade even when the prompt seems correct. More instructions are not always better. Reference management, clear evaluation criteria, batch review and model selection often matter as much as the prompt itself. A usable production system therefore needs governance around the generation step—not just access to the model.

The principle that transfers

Start with a real creative need. Prototype narrowly. Make quality criteria visible. Keep humans responsible for judgment. Document the pattern only after it has survived production use.

Responsible Production

Efficiency matters. Trust matters more.

Generative AI belongs inside the same professional standards that govern the rest of a production.

Human accountability

A person remains responsible for the creative, factual and ethical decision.

Approved inputs

Reference and source assets should be appropriate for the intended use and review context.

Visible quality criteria

Teams need shared standards for acceptance, not vague reactions to generated output.

Right tool / no tool

AI is not automatically the best answer. A sound workflow includes knowing when not to use it.
